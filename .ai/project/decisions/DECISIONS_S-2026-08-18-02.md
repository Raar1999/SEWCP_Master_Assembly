## DEC-22 — `DEC-21` is reopened and superseded; disposition **B — sanitize before publication**

**Question.** `DEC-21` ruled *preserve unchanged* for the local Windows account name carried in
ten tracked CAD run records. An independent review of `S-2026-08-18-02` measured its factual
basis and found it wrong in both halves. Does the ruling stand?

**Decision. It does not stand. `DEC-21` is superseded by this entry.** The disposition is
**B — lawfully sanitize before publication**, filed as `ECR-D-018`.

**Grounds, each measured and none asserted.**

1. **The count was wrong.** `DEC-21` records *"41 times"*. The measured figure is **52** in the
   ten run records across three path forms, plus **1** in `DEC-21`'s own prose — **53**. The
   missing eleven are the fully-backslashed form, which appears in the raw JSON as
   `Users\\<user>`. This is `OI-V-17`'s class exactly: a figure asserted rather than measured.
2. **The name is not already in the commit history.** Every commit is authored and committed as
   `Raar1999 <91361865+Raar1999@users.noreply.github.com>`, a GitHub noreply address. The
   account name is **net-new** information that publication would introduce.
3. **The repository is not public.** `DEC-21` opens *"The repository is public."* `STATE.md`,
   authoritative at B3, records PRIVATE with an unclosed owner gate. The disclosure has **not**
   happened and is still fully reversible.

**Why the balance changes.** `DEC-21`'s evidentiary objection is sound and is not overturned:
editing a verbatim capture is a real cost. But it was weighed against a disclosure believed to
be already made and irreversible. Substituting the true facts, the cost is unchanged and the
benefit is no longer zero: the remedy is available now, is bounded, and becomes permanently
unavailable at the moment visibility flips. **Cheap and effective now; expensive and useless
later.** That asymmetry is the whole decision.

**What is preserved, expressly.** The ten run records remain, in place, with every failure they
record intact. `DEC-21` remains on disk, readable and citable, **superseded rather than deleted
and never re-worded**. No failure is hidden, softened or removed. `DEC-21` was right that these
are tracked failure evidence and this decision does not disturb that finding.

## DEC-23 — the rank-1 override of `LAW-07` clauses 6, 7 and 8

**Question.** `LAW-07` clause 6 states *"Published history is never rewritten"*; clause 7,
*"Tags are annotated and never moved"*; clause 8, *"Force push is prohibited unless explicitly
authorised by the framework."* `V-22`, severity **BLOCKING**, restates clause 6 as a checked
property. The act `DEC-22` requires engages all three. May it proceed?

**Decision. Yes, on the owner's rank-1 instruction, recorded — not assumed, and not by
reinterpreting the law.**

**`core/PRECEDENCE.md`** is a total ordering: rank 1 is live human instruction, rank 4 is the
engineering laws. Rank 1 governs. `PRECEDENCE` §4 and `LAW-10` clause 4 require the override to
be **recorded as an approval artifact before dependent work is committed**; that record is
[`APR-039`](../approvals/APR-039_Rank-1_Override_LAW-07_For_ECR-D-018_Sanitization.md), filed
in the same act, bound to `ECR-D-018` by DC-1.

**The precedent is this repository's own.** `OQ-14` — the Stage 6 canonical emission — was
authorised by the owner at `DECISIONS_S-2026-08-11-06` DEC-10 notwithstanding two undischarged
items, and the emission was performed and recorded as what happened. Same instrument, same
rank, same discipline of writing down what was overridden instead of arguing it away.

**Three things this override is not.**

- **It is not a weakening of a check.** `V-22` has no executable implementation in this
  repository — consistent with `CMP-BLOCK-005`, the absent validation-campaign infrastructure —
  so nothing is disabled, relaxed, deleted or edited to obtain a green result. The law is
  overridden in the open by the only authority that outranks it, and the fact that no machine
  would have caught it is stated here rather than relied upon.
- **It is not a reinterpretation.** Whether clause 6's *"published"* reaches a private remote is
  genuinely undefined and is raised as [`ECR-Q-016`](../ecr/ECR-Q-016_Whether_Published_History_In_LAW-07_Means_Pushed_Or_Public.md)
  under `LAW-12`. The outcome is invariant under both readings — under the narrow one no
  override is needed, under the broad one this override supplies it — so the query blocks
  nothing and is left for the `chief-systems-engineer` to rule.
- **It is not authority to push.** Clause 8 says *by the framework*, and no framework
  authorisation is claimed. **No push is performed.** The force-update is computed, reported and
  left at the publication gate for the owner.

**What is preserved through the rewrite, and verified afterwards rather than asserted:** author
identity, committer identity, author and committer timestamps, commit messages, tag annotations
with their tagger and date, file modes, paths, and every byte of every file except the approved
account-name substitution. `LAW-07` clauses 1–5 — no AI attribution, no co-author trailer, no
generated-by trailer, identity never modified, configured identity never overwritten — are
**not** overridden and are not touched.

## DEC-24 — scope of the substitution, and the two consequences it carries

**Question.** Exactly which bytes are in scope, and what does changing them break?

**Decision — three parts.**

**(a) The account-name token only, in all three path forms, everywhere in reachable history.**
The separator is captured and re-emitted verbatim, so `/`, `\` and the JSON-escaped `\\` each
survive as they were. Fifty-two occurrences across ten run records. Nothing else in any
traceback is touched.

**(b) The single occurrence in `DEC-21`'s own prose is in scope.** The owner's instruction sets
the objective as **zero** occurrences of the account name in public-reachable content, and one
surviving occurrence identifies exactly as well as fifty-two. The token is quoted as a code
span; replacing it with the same neutral placeholder used everywhere else leaves the sentence's
meaning complete. This is recorded here, prominently, precisely because the owner's instruction
also says `DEC-21` *"must remain historical evidence. Do not silently mutate it."* **It is not
silent: it is declared in the decision that supersedes it, in `ECR-D-018` §5, and in the ledger
entry that closes the session.** `DEC-21` is not deleted, not re-worded and not softened; one
personal identifier inside it is normalised exactly as it is normalised in every other file.

**(c) `R-031` will stale, and is corrected by supersession, never by mutation.**
`.ai/project/decisions/DECISIONS_S-2026-08-17-01.md` is pinned by `R-031` — the only `CURRENT`
result — at DC-1 `d0af3de0…0ecc5bd3`. Changing its bytes moves that digest and `X-06` fails.
**That is the drift detector working and it is not to be silenced.** `EXECUTION_ARCHITECTURE.md`
§6.1 gives the remedy in one sentence: correction is by supersession. `R-032` republishes the
pins and seals `R-031` at the DC-1 it then stands at. This is the identical mechanism, invoked
for the identical reason, as the `R-030 → R-031` supersession one session earlier.

**What is out of scope and stays that way.** Traceback structure, exception types, exception
messages, module names, line numbers, timestamps, run ids, operation ids, Fusion build
identifiers, every other field of every run record, and every engineering artifact in the
repository. The five superseded results that also pin the `S-2026-08-17-01` decisions file —
`R-025`, `R-026`, `R-028`, `R-029`, `R-030` — are historical and are correctly left stale; `X-06`
enforces currency on `CURRENT` results, which is why it passes today with `R-030` already
holding a superseded pin.
